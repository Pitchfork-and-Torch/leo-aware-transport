# Results interpretation

Generated by `python -m experiments.run_suite` (re-run to refresh numbers in `results/summary.csv`).

## Qualitative takeaways

### Under LEO dynamics

1. **CUBIC under-utilizes.** Mobility loss bursts are treated as congestion, so cwnd collapses after handovers and recovery is slow relative to capacity.
2. **BBR-family approx pushes high goodput** via delivery-rate max-filter, but often with **higher p95 RTT** and multi-flow loss when min-RTT / BDP are wrong across epochs.
3. **LeoAware** aims for the middle of the throughput-latency Pareto:
   - Endpoint reconfiguration detection + sample invalidation
   - Soft re-probe instead of CUBIC collapse
   - Mobility loss does not automatically cut cwnd

### Fast handovers (`leo_fast_ho`)

This is the stress case. LeoAware should be competitive or better on goodput while keeping p95 RTT closer to usable interactive ranges than a pure max-BW pusher.

### Multi-flow (`leo_multi`)

Shared buffer + aggressive model-based CCAs can explode loss. Jain fairness is reported in `results/leo_multi_fairness.csv`. Prefer interpreting multi-flow as a fairness/loss stress test, not just sum goodput.

### Terrestrial control

LeoAware must not catastrophically regress vs CUBIC/BBR on a stable path. Competitive goodput at similar RTT is the pass bar.

## Reading the plots

- Orange vertical lines = handovers
- cwnd (MSS) dips after hops for CUBIC (hard) vs LeoAware (soft re-probe)
- Goodput time series shows recovery speed after each reconfiguration
- Throughput-latency scatter: lower-right is better (high goodput, low p95 RTT)

## Honesty

These are **simulator** results with simplified dynamics and educational baselines. They are a research starting point, not a claim of production superiority over full BBRv3 or kernel CUBIC on real Starlink paths.
